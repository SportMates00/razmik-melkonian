Song and Piano Recital

Birdsongs

Songs of Nature and the Human Soul

Among nature’s most captivating sounds, birdsong has long inspired composers and poets, becoming a powerful symbol of freedom, beauty and the profound connection between nature and the human soul. This programme explores the many ways in which birds have found their voice in classical music, particularly through the intimate pairing of piano and voice.

Each work captures the distinctive character of a bird, weaving a rich tapestry of sound that evokes both the beauty of birdsong and its deeper metaphorical meanings. From the purity and simplicity of folk-inspired songs by Komitas and Brahms to the exotic colours of Russian vocal music, and from works inspired by Greek mythology to songs reflecting the universal symbolism of birds across cultures, the programme offers a diverse and evocative musical journey.

Through the imagination of some of classical music’s most celebrated composers, these songs bring to life the enduring fascination with birds, revealing their many guises as symbols of freedom, longing, mystery and transcendence. Together, they celebrate the beauty of the natural world and its timeless power to inspire the human spirit.

Piano Programme

Composers at Crossroads

This programme explores piano music written by composers in their late twenties, a period of life that unfolds differently for each of them. While Schubert was approaching the final years of his life, Scriabin was undergoing a transformation, moving away from his early Chopinesque influences and beginning to find his own musical language. Chopin, meanwhile, was navigating fragile health while creating works that would become cornerstones of the piano repertoire for generations to come. Rachmaninoff, emerging from a period of creative crisis, was rebuilding his confidence as a composer while facing financial pressures.

In different ways, these works reflect the personal circumstances and artistic challenges of their creators, offering glimpses into the lives behind the music. Through them, the programme explores the different crossroads that composers encounter, and the ways in which life experiences can shape an artistic voice.
